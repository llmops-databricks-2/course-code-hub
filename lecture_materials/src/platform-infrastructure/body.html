LLMOps course · Platform lecture · Infrastructure

What happens between your click and the answer, and who built the road it travels?

Every lecture so far started inside a workspace that was already there: your user, your schemas, your warehouse, your repository and three deploy service principals. This page goes underneath. It follows one action from your browser through Databricks and back, then walks the platform top to bottom: the account and its identities, Unity Catalog, GitHub and the trust between them, the tools that manage it all, and why the course can rebuild the whole workspace from code.

The names, grants and steps are the course's own, from the course-assistant repository (setup script, onboarding and runbook) as of November 2026. Every simulator runs in your browser and changes nothing in Databricks or GitHub.

The big picture

A day on the platform

Code comes in from the left, through GitHub; people's work comes in from the right. Both meet at the platform's front door, and every request is authenticated, then authorized, before compute reads storage in parallel. Ten short chapters, then the whole day at once.     4GitHub and Databricks

    How a workflow becomes a service principal without a secret

    GitHub signs a short-lived token about each job. Each of your three deploy principals has one federation policy that accepts exactly one subject. Change where the job runs and see which principal, if any, the token becomes.

  7Student onboarding
  
    One daily run, many students, every step idempotent
    
  
    The onboarding runs once a day as sp-onboarding-llmops. For every student on the list it takes each step that is not done yet; a step looks up by name before it creates, and a failed step holds only the steps that need its result. Set what goes wrong, then run a few days.
